# Ideation Workflow 🔨

**Where it sits:** the **bridge between the diamonds** — it picks up the Opportunity Workflow's stack-ranked HMWs and carries the team from problem-alignment into solution-divergence, then hands whatever survives to the [Solution Workflow](../solution/).

**What it produces:** a shortlist of **candidate solution directions** — generated workshop-style, off-canvas. Indicative, not prioritised.

---

> ## ✋ Read this first — the one place I deliberately *under-index* on AI
>
> Most of this repo is me handing work to an AI. **This workflow is the one place I hand it back.**
>
> Ideation is the most fun part of product development, and it's the team's release valve. The first diamond is cerebral, taxing, and mildly adversarial — PMs, EMs and designers grinding on an opportunity, and shit gets heated. Ideation is where people get to be a human being and release some steam. And frankly, in my experience, this is — for some reason — where lasting team relationships get built. Humans are funny like that. So this is where I *let the team take it easy and live a little.* Fetch a piece of paper, fold it into eight, set a timer, make a mess. And if your designer has a strong opinion on how to run it — nine times out of ten it's the designer — let them run it.
>
> **Not everything needs an AI workflow.** Automating this end to end would suck the soul out of the one part of the process people actually enjoy. So the AI here does the boring bit — sets the stage, explains the exercise, protects the timebox — and then gets out of the way. Have a bit of fun in life. That's the whole point.

---

> **👐 Defaults, not doctrine.** Crazy 8s, brainwriting, and dot voting are my go-tos, not the law. Any UX designer worth their salt has strong, hard-won opinions on how to ideate — and a big part of the PM's job at this stage is to *make space for other people to bring their own way of doing it.* Hold the frame — the timebox, quantity over quality, democratising the room — but don't police the technique.

---

## The AI's role flips depending on who's in the room

| Mode | The AI is… | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Team** | a **facilitator**, never a participant | Sets up the exercise, teaches anyone who's new to it, protects the conditions — then shuts up. It contributes **zero ideas**; the ideas belong to the team. |
| **Solo** | a **sparring partner** | No team to crowd out, so now it actually ideates *with* you — its own eight ideas against yours. |

---

## Team mode — Crazy 8s + dot vote

The classic, and still underrated. Eight ideas, eight minutes, one folded sheet — quantity over quality, judgment deferred, the timebox doing the work of silencing your inner critic. Then a dot vote to surface the room's favourites.

**What the AI does:** it builds a **Miro board that teaches the exercise** — the fold, the timer, *why* there's time pressure, why "bad" ideas are exactly the point — so someone who's never run a Crazy 8 understands why it works before the clock starts.

- **Co-located?** Do it on paper with real dot stickers; use the board as the explainer.
- **Distributed?** Run it on the board itself.

**And then it stops.** The AI sets up the Crazy 8s frame and the dot-vote frame, and hands the room over. It does **not** tally the dots, cluster the ideas, or tell you what they "mean" — counting your own stickers and arguing about what's on the wall *is* the teamwork, and that's precisely the bit worth protecting. *(That's my call on how far the AI goes; I'm happy to be argued out of it.)*

## Brainwriting — 🚧 coming soon

The other technique I rate, parked for now because doing it justice is more involved. Capturing the intent so it isn't lost: **brainwriting democratises ideation.** Everyone writes in silence and passes their sheet on, building on each other's ideas — which forces collaboration, diffuses the loud, opinionated stakeholder (again, usually the designer 😉), and lets the quieter, less well-spoken, or more timid people get a word in edgeways. When it's built, it lives here.

## Solo mode — dueling Crazy 8s

Solo ideation is a perfectly legitimate way to work, so it gets a real mode, not a lesser one. Here the AI stops facilitating and starts playing:

1. **Blind.** You do your eight. The AI does its eight. **Neither sees the other's first** — no anchoring, no polite drift toward the obvious answer.
2. **Reveal.** Both hands on the table at once. You each describe what you came up with.
3. **Negotiate.** Talk it through — combine, kill, spin off, steal the good bits.
4. **A quick two-way dot vote** — you and the AI, gut-feel favourites. **Purely indicative, and explicitly so:** it's a temperature check, not a decision. Prioritisation is the PM's job, made deliberately downstream — not something a two-person straw poll gets to settle.

## Hand-off

Whatever survives — team or solo — is a set of **candidate solution directions**, not a plan. They cross into diamond two as the raw material for the [Solution Workflow](../solution/).
